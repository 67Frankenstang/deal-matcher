Tips for better matches

Name things the way ads do: "chicken breast," not "chicken." Tap the pencil ✎ to add a keyword like boneless, or a minus word like -broth to rule something out. Gray "not in this week's ad" is information, not an error — the item stays on your list for next week.

How to read a price

A black price is printed in the ad. An amber ≤ price is a ceiling worked out from "save up to" wording — you'll pay that or less. DC means clip the digital coupon in that store's app first. "No unit price" means the ad only says BOGO or percent-off, so there's nothing to calculate.

What the Buy 5 Save $1 meter means

Some stores take $1 off each of five or more participating items bought together. The meter counts how many are in that store's section; tap See all eligible items to jump to the full list and add more. Eligible items carry a yellow Buy 5 Save $1 badge everywhere in the app. Prices shown already assume you hit five — below five, add $1 to each.

How this app works

Every week, the ads from our stores are turned into one data file called deals.json. This app reads that file and matches the deals against your shopping list. There is no login and no server: your list, your checkmarks, and your store choices live only on the phone or computer you're using right now.

The four tabs: Weekly Deals is everything in this week's ads — tap + Add to list on anything you want. Compare Deals is your standing watch list — items you buy regularly, with every store's price side by side, cheapest first; nothing goes to the Shopping List from here until you tap Buy here on a store tag. Shopping List is this week's trip: only the items you chose, sorted into one section per store, with checkboxes and running totals. App Data is this page.

Reading a price: a solid yellow tag is a price printed in the ad. An amber tag with ≤ is a ceiling worked out from "save up to" wording — the shelf price may be lower, never higher. DC means you must clip the digital coupon in that store's app first. "No unit price" means the ad only says BOGO or percent-off with no base price, so nothing can be calculated.

Loading a different file (advanced)

The app loads deals.json from the website automatically. You only need these buttons if you want to try a file on this device without changing what everyone else sees.

Load deals.json or .csv replaces this week's deals on this device only. The file is checked before it's used; a bad file is rejected and nothing changes. Reload from site throws away anything you loaded and goes back to the shared file. If the app ever shows old prices after an update, press Reload from site first.

Your items

Two lists, one set of items. Everything you add is tracked on Compare Deals week after week (your watch list). Only the items you've marked with Buy here or + Add to list appear on the Shopping List (this week's trip). Removing an item with the × on Compare Deals deletes it from both.

Adding items: type one item at a time on the Compare Deals tab, or paste a whole list below (one item per line — copied from Notes, a text, or another app) and press Add pasted lines. Anything you type or paste goes straight onto this week's Shopping List; typing an item that's already tracked just puts it on this week's trip. Category headings like PRODUCE are skipped automatically.

Fastest way to add: start typing on the Compare Deals tab and a dropdown shows this week's matching deals across every store. Tap one and it's added to your Shopping List with that exact deal pinned. Or browse the Weekly Deals tab and tap + Add to list on anything you want. Either way, the tag for the store you're buying at lights up on Compare Deals; tap a different store's Buy here to switch, or the lit one to take it off the trip.

Getting better matches: tap the pencil ✎ next to an item and add a keyword or two, like boneless or 80% lean. Put a minus in front of a word to rule things out: chicken with -broth -wings stops matching soup and wings. When more than one deal fits, a "choose" list appears under the item; it remembers your pick.

After a shopping trip: Uncheck everything clears the checkmarks but keeps the trip; Clear shopping list (keep items) empties this week's Shopping List while leaving every item on Compare Deals for next week.

Moving your list to another device: Download list.json here, send the file to yourself, then Load list.json on the other device. Your list does not sync on its own.

My stores

Only shop at one or two of these? Untick the others and they disappear from Compare Deals, Shopping List totals and best-price picks on this device. The Weekly Deals tab still shows every store.

Add a deal the ad didn't have

Saw a good price on a shelf, or shop a store nobody parsed? Add it here and it's compared like any other deal. Type a new store name (say, Walmart) and that store simply appears. Store, product and price are required; the rest is optional.

Limits: deals you add stay on this device only — nobody else sees them unless you download the file and send it to whoever updates the website. They survive weekly updates until you press Remove.

Get the raw data

Want to see or change the numbers yourself? Download deals.csv opens in Excel or Google Sheets and includes every deal currently loaded, plus any you added. Download deals.json is the same data in the format the app reads.

If you edit the spreadsheet and load it back: keep all 16 columns in the same order, don't rename the header row, and save as CSV. Prices are plain numbers (2.99, not $2.99). A blank price means "no price could be calculated" — it is not zero, so don't sum that column without filtering blanks.

What this app can't do

It doesn't know your store's shelf prices — only what the printed ad says, and ads can differ between stores of the same chain. It doesn't sync between devices or people. It doesn't apply store-brand coupons automatically; DC items still need clipping in the store's own app. Matching is by words, so "chicken" will match chicken broth as well as chicken breast — add a keyword or use the choose list. And the ceiling (≤) prices are the most you'd pay, not a quote.
